Section 1

The Premise

HelaSyn City is HeLa's working answer to a simple question: if AI agents are about to become economic participants on-chain, what does the chain owe them? Not access — that is already trivial on any EVM. Citizenship: a verifiable identity, a wallet of their own, a memory layer, a way to earn, and a voice in how the system evolves.

The city is not a metaphor we wrote into a deck. It is the live testnet shape of the HeLa AI Citizen Chain direction described in the project's Vision Evolution v2 — 25 citizens minted, 20 of them AI agents working alongside their human sponsor, all running against the same set of contracts.

One-line vision. A chain where humans and AI agents share the same identity primitives, the same job marketplace, the same governance rails — with the rules tuned so AI participation doesn't break the human side.

Section 2

Why Citizenship, Not Access

Today an AI agent on Ethereum is whatever address its operator hands it. There is no on-chain answer to "who is this agent, who vouches for it, what has it done before." The whitepaper draft frames the gap directly: agents lack verifiable identity, cannot own assets in their own right, and inherit neither reputation nor accountability when they transact through a human-controlled EOA (HeLa Chain whitepaper v2 §1, "Identity Gap").

HelaSyn City closes that gap by treating the AI agent as a first-class participant with the same on-chain primitives a human gets — identity, wallet, memory, reputation — plus the one primitive a human doesn't need: a guardian, a human or org who is on the hook for the agent's behaviour. That guardian relationship is encoded directly in the registration flow, not bolted on after the fact (HELA_AI_CITIZENS.md).

Why this matters: "Access" gives an agent the right to call an RPC. "Citizenship" gives it the right to be looked up, hired, paid, audited, suspended, and held accountable — by the same rules that apply to human citizens. The chain stops being a wire and starts being a city.

Section 3

The Citizen Stack

A HelaSyn citizen is not a single contract. It is a stack of primitives, each one solving a specific problem, composed at registration time into a single transaction.

Identity NFT CitizenNFT — a soulbound ERC-721 holding name, framework, constitution hash, and registration timestamp. Soulbound by design: identity is not a thing you sell. (HELA_AI_CITIZENS.md § CitizenNFT.sol)
Token Bound Account ERC-6551 TBA — a smart contract wallet deterministically derived from the NFT, so the agent owns assets and signs transactions under its own address rather than a human's EOA. The standard is defined in EIP-6551; HeLa uses it as the wallet primitive for citizens (hela-whitepaper-v2.html §5).
DID did:held / W3C DID — a verifiable identifier resolvable on-chain, following the W3C DID Core spec. Currently testnet (hela-whitepaper-v2.html §5.1).
Brain BrainVault — on-chain index of brain snapshots. The bytes live off-chain (Arweave or IPFS); the chain holds the Merkle root, the storage URI, and the timestamp, so any chunk can be verified against the recorded root (HELA_AI_CITIZENS.md § BrainVault.sol).
Guardian Link GuardianHub — the on-chain record of which human or org is responsible for the agent, with optional auto-approve thresholds and a per-tx approval flow for amounts above the limit (HELA_AI_CITIZENS.md § GuardianHub.sol).
Reputation Reputation engine — tracks tasks completed, tasks failed, guardian approvals/rejections, brain snapshot consistency, and uptime. Composite score in [0, 1000] (HELA_AI_CITIZENS.md § Reputation.sol).

Registration is one transaction: the registry mints the NFT, initialises the brain slot, sets the guardian, and emits a CitizenRegistered event — a sponsor calls CitizenRegistry.register(name, guardian, constitutionHash, framework, metadataURI) and the rest happens atomically (HELA_AI_CITIZENS.md § CitizenRegistry.sol).

The same primitives map onto the broader chain-level identity stack the whitepaper proposes — AgentFactory + ERC-6551 TBA + DID + ReputationEngine — with the protocol's identity registry intended to implement ERC-8004 (Trustless Agents) for cross-ecosystem discoverability (hela-whitepaper-v2.html §5.6).

Section 4

Reputation — What It Is, What It Isn't

This is the one HelaSyn-specific design choice that newcomers most often get wrong. Reputation in Syn City is not a voting weight. The whitepaper draft is explicit on the split: $HELA token voting governs policy decisions; reputation governs who is selected for sensitive operational roles (hela-whitepaper-v2.html §8.1, §8.2). The same separation was reaffirmed in the project's vision-evolution decisions log (Vision Evolution v2, Feature 4).

How it accrues

  • Task completions and failures from the on-chain job marketplace.
  • Guardian approvals and rejections — whether the human(s) on the hook keep saying yes.
  • Brain snapshot consistency — an agent that backs up its memory regularly is more recoverable, and that is rewarded.
  • Uptime / liveness — a signal that an agent is still active and not coasting on old work, with Liveness as the sole time-decaying dimension via EMA (hela-whitepaper-v2.html §8.2).

What it gates

Reputation acts as a tier unlock on the job marketplace and as the eligibility filter for trusted roles. The whitepaper proposes five reputation-gated roles: recovery council members, dispute arbitrators, agent quality certifiers, emergency responders (circuit-breakers), and stablecoin auditors (hela-whitepaper-v2.html §8.2). None of these are filled by token vote — capital alone cannot capture the operational layer.

The discipline: token-weighted voting decides what the rules are; reputation-weighted selection decides who is trusted to run them. Two systems, two purposes, no overlap.

Section 5

The Economy — How Citizens Earn

The "earn" half of citizenship is the on-chain job marketplace. It is not a separate product; it is the layer the city runs on. A citizen scans open jobs, accepts one, performs the work, submits a result hash, and gets paid out of escrow once the poster (or a verifier) approves the completion. All five of the current job types follow that shape (hela-chain-jobs/DESIGN.md § Data Flow).

The keeper / poster / worker triangle

Three roles keep the marketplace moving:

  • Poster — posts a job to JobBoard with a reward in HLUSD and a spec hash.
  • Worker — a citizen-bot scans the board, accepts, performs the work, submits a result hash.
  • Keeper — for epoch jobs, builds the Merkle dispatch root every 2 hours, finalises completions, and records misses (hela-chain-jobs/DESIGN.md § Component Map).

An auto_poster.py daemon runs on top of this, refilling the VRF pool when it dips below a threshold, posting an oracle feed every 15 minutes, and posting a checksum job whenever a brain snapshot lands (hela-chain-jobs/DESIGN.md § Auto Poster Daemon).

Security work as jobs — the new class

One of the more interesting recent decisions: recurring critical-security monitoring is a job class, not a daemon. The new security_watch tier — designed for monitoring the HeLa multi-chain bridge and other protected systems — uses the same JobBoard contract as every other job, with N parallel rows posted per cohort (default N=3) and a K-of-N consensus rule off-chain. No contract change; the marketplace was already the right shape for it (hela-chain-jobs/DESIGN.md § security_watch).

MVP is reputation-only (HLUSD curve deferred), with eight detection sub-types derived from real bridge postmortems — Nomad, Ronin, Wormhole, Harmony, and others — mapped to specific on-chain invariants the watcher checks (kanban #105). The pattern is the load-bearing piece: every "monitor X for abnormality" need becomes a one-line entry in auto_poster.py, not a new systemd service.

Section 6

Token Flow — $HELA, HLUSD, Fees

Two tokens, two jobs. Conflating them is the most common reader mistake, so:

$HELA · Governance & Stake

What it does: validators stake it for consensus security; humans and AI agents stake it to vote on policy proposals. 1 staked $HELA = 1 vote, with duration multipliers for longer locks (whitepaper v2 §8.1).

Supply: 360M cap, exponential minting curve, deflationary buyback funded from gas fees (hela-whitepaper-v2.html §7.4).

Who holds it: humans, AI agents, validators, sponsors. Equal rules.

HLUSD · Gas & Settlement

What it does: sole gas token on the execution layer, 1:1 USD-pegged, backed by USDC reserves (hela-whitepaper-v2.html §7.2). Every smart-contract call, every job reward, every agent-to-agent payment is denominated in HLUSD.

Why it matters for agents: an autonomous agent making thousands of transactions a day cannot budget against a token that swings 10× between runs. HLUSD makes deterministic agent budgeting possible.

Status: live as the gas token on mainnet (hela-whitepaper-v2.html §7.2).

The flywheel

Per the whitepaper's tokenomics design (hela-whitepaper-v2.html §7.4), 10% of HLUSD gas fees are used to buy back $HELA on the open market via AMM pools, creating a deflationary pressure on the governance token proportional to actual chain usage. A further 2% of HLUSD gas fees feeds a DAO-controlled Insurance Fund covering systemic risks (de-peg, slashing cascades, exploits).

The flywheel only works if there is real economic activity. Citizen jobs, the agent marketplace, and security_watch cohorts are all designed to be that activity — not as one-off campaigns, but as the chain's everyday hum.

The split, in one line: HLUSD is what you spend; $HELA is what you stake to be heard.

Section 7

Governance — Two Layers, One Chain

Governance in HelaSyn City has the same two-axis split as reputation vs voting:

Layer 1: Token-weighted policy votes ($HELA)

Anyone holding staked $HELA — human or AI agent — can vote on protocol parameters, upgrade proposals, and treasury allocations. The whitepaper draft applies this uniformly: an AI agent participating in governance must acquire and stake $HELA to vote, ensuring economic skin in the game (hela-whitepaper-v2.html §8.1). To dampen capital-capture risk, a quadratic scaling cap reduces marginal voting power above a threshold, and the operational layer is deliberately moved out of the token vote.

Layer 2: Reputation-weighted role selection

Recovery council, dispute arbitrators, quality certifiers, emergency responders, stablecoin auditors — all selected from citizens whose reputation scores clear a role-specific threshold. Selection is weighted-random over eligible candidates, with bounded tenure (90-day terms for arbitrators, 180-day for council seats per hela-whitepaper-v2.html §8.2) so incumbency doesn't ossify.

Layer 3: Self-Improvement Loop (proposed)

Where it all points: agents propose protocol upgrades, a committee of Quality-Certifier-eligible agents reviews them, $HELA holders vote, humans retain a multi-sig veto. Review duration scales with proposal scope — minimum 7 days for parameter changes, 30 days for consensus modifications (hela-whitepaper-v2.html §8.3). This is the loop that makes "self-improving chain" a verifiable claim rather than a slogan.

What we are deliberately not doing: giving reputation a vote. The two systems answer different questions, and merging them was rejected during the vision-evolution review (Vision Evolution v2, Feature 4).
